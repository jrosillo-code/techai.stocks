Does anything here actually behave differently?

The most useful result of the previous round was a negative one: nearly every idea tested turned out to be the same bet in different clothing. Two new families were added to attack that directly. This is whether they worked.

Yes — but only by not being permanently long. The hedged and market-neutral strategies sit at a median correlation of 0.09. The best long-only family manages 0.27, and most are far higher.

That gap is the whole finding, and it is not subtle. Every earlier attempt to find something “different” kept the long-only constraint, so the co-movement it was trying to escape was built into it. Nothing about technology stocks was ever the cause. The cost is real: a hedged book pays borrow every day, trades two sides instead of one, and gives up most of the raw return — check it against the benchmarks before treating low correlation as a good thing on its own.
