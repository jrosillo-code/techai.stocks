Do these ideas work well together?

Two mediocre strategies can beat a good one if they fail at different times. This page checks whether they actually do — how closely they move together, whether they collapse in the same market conditions, and whether combining them helps.

These numbers are from a simulated market, not the real one. The system has been built and audited, but the study against real market prices has not run yet. Everything here demonstrates that the machinery works — none of it says anything about how these ideas would have actually performed.

Does anything here actually behave differently?

The most useful result of the previous round was a negative one: nearly every idea tested turned out to be the same bet in different clothing. Two new families were added to attack that directly. This is whether they worked.

The honest answer: mostly no. The two families added specifically to break this pattern land at a median correlation of 0.81, against 0.74 for the families that already existed — worse, not better. The sleeve built only to find the least-alike corner of the sector still moves with the index at 0.83. On this evidence the sector does not contain an independent corner to hide in, and the one thing that did help was reducing exposure, not re-picking stocks.
